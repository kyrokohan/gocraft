# Skyspear: the design

gocraft is turning into **Skyspear**, a prehistoric lost world. This page is the plan. The
[README](../README.md) says what is in the game today.

The block-building game as it was, under its old name, is tagged `v0.1.0`.

## What stays true

- It runs in a terminal, on [glonk](https://glonk.dev).
- Survival and creative both stay.
- Everything is the game's own: names, art and sounds, made by code in this repo. The
  sources are real animals and real stone-age craft, not other games or films.
- Cave people and dinosaurs lived 66 million years apart. Here they share a world on
  purpose.

## The game in one breath

Night. You crouch by a fire you carried here as an ember in a clay pot. Past the edge of
its light, eyes shine and wait. Far off something heavy walks, and you feel it before you
hear it. Over the black line of the fire mountain hangs the Skyspear, a little longer
tonight than last night.

## Five pillars

1. **You hear it before you see it.** A giant's footfall shakes the view. A hunter's low
   rumble comes through the ferns. A falling star flashes, and its boom comes seconds
   later. Fear is made by sound.
2. **Hunted, then hunter.** You begin with bare hands, hiding in the ferns. You end with
   a sky-iron spear, a beast under you and people at your fire.
3. **Fire is everything.** You can't make it at first: you find it, carry it and keep it
   alive. Fire cooks, hardens spears, warms, gives light and holds the dark back.
4. **The best things lie in the worst places.** Obsidian is only on the fire mountain.
   Iron only falls from the sky, into craters that are still burning.
5. **The Skyspear is coming.** The comet grows each night. Everyone in the world, you
   too, is getting ready for the night it falls.

## The world: one lost world, all ages

Each land is an age of the Earth, with its own plants and beasts. There's no grass in
the old lands: the ground is moss and fern.

| Land | Plants | Beasts | You go there for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fern prairie | ferns, horsetails, stump palms | mossbacks, bugler herds, shieldbrows, nippers | food, fiber, your first camp |
| Umbrella-pine highland | umbrella pines, fan-leaf trees, giant redwoods | thornbacks, cloudgrazers | wood, resin, high ground |
| Tree-fern jungle | tree ferns, vines, the first flowers | quickclaw packs, sailjaws at the rivers | hides, eggs, danger |
| Coal swamp | scale trees, giant horsetails, black water | rattlewings, logcrawlers, sunbacks | peat and coal, amber |
| Badlands | almost none | stiltwings, boulderbacks | flint, ochre, great skeletons |
| Ashlands and the fire mountain | none | almost none | obsidian, firestone, fire itself |
| Mammoth steppe | snow, dwarf trees | snowtusks, daggercats, howlers, slowclaws | furs, tusks, cold that kills |
| Shores and seas | reeds, shells | galewings, lakenecks, leapfins, deepmaws | fish, shells, cliffs with nests |

Places to find, set by the seed:

- Great skeletons with ribs like a hall.
- Tar pits that trap anything that walks in.
- Hot springs with colored rings, and geysers that throw you up.
- Lava tubes that are ready-made caves, and old craters with lakes in them.
- The camps of other people.

A fire mountain can always be found: its smoke by day and its glow by night show on the
horizon from far beyond the view distance.

## The beasts

Twenty-two kinds, each with the name a cave person would give it.

| Cave name | The real animal | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| mossback | the game's own | grazes; the first beast you tame |
| nipper | Compsognathus | a flock of hen-sized thieves that take food from your hand |
| bugler | Parasaurolophus | herds; calls like a horn across the valley; gives the alarm |
| shieldbrow | Triceratops | a small family; charges what comes near its young |
| thornback | Stegosaurus | alone; swings a spiked tail |
| boulderback | Ankylosaurus | slow, armored, with a club for a tail |
| cloudgrazer | a long-necked giant | 14 blocks tall; the ground shakes; it doesn't notice you |
| quickclaw | Deinonychus | feathered pack hunter of the night |
| dread | Tyrannosaurus | the top hunter; a rumble you feel; you hide |
| sailjaw | Spinosaurus | waits in rivers |
| sunback | Dimetrodon | slow in the cold morning, fast at noon |
| snowtusk, daggercat, howler, slowclaw | mammoth, sabertooth, dire wolf, ground sloth | the life of the cold steppe |
| galewing, stiltwing | Pteranodon, Quetzalcoatlus | one soars and dips for fish, one stalks on the ground |
| lakeneck, leapfin, deepmaw | plesiosaur, ichthyosaur, mosasaur | the sea: shy, playful, deadly |
| rattlewing, logcrawler | giant dragonfly, giant millipede | the swamp: harmless, strange, loud |

How they live, which is what makes the world feel alive:

- **Herds are real.** A herd moves together between grazing and water, with the young in
  the middle. One alarm call sets the whole herd running, and a stampede can kill.
- **Hunters hunt the herds, not only you.** You can watch a hunt, steal from the kill, or
  lead a hunter into a family of shieldbrows.
- **They see, hear and smell.** Crouching in tall ferns hides you. Running is loud. Raw
  meat in your pack draws hunters.
- **They fear fire.** A burning hearth keeps most beasts off. The dread only fears a
  big one.
- **At night you see only their eyes.** This takes the place of the gloam.

## The cave person

- A body made of boxes, in hides, with what you hold in its hand. It walks, swings,
  crouches and throws.
- **V** changes the view: through your eyes, or from behind your shoulder. The camera
  from behind pulls in when a wall is in the way.
- **C** crouches: slower, quieter, hidden in tall plants.
- Health, food and air stay. **Warmth** joins them, and only matters in the cold lands
  and the Long Winter.

## Tools: from flint to sky iron

| Tier | Made of | Found | What it's like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | wood and bone | anywhere | club, digging stick, spear with a fire-hardened tip |
| 2 | flint | chalk seams, badlands, river beds | knapped: hand axe, knife, spear point, pick |
| 3 | obsidian | the fire mountain only | the sharpest edge, and the first to break |
| 4 | sky iron | craters only | hammered cold on a stone; the strongest, and lasts longest |

- **Weapons:**
  - A club knocks back and stuns.
  - A spear reaches a block farther and can be thrown.
  - A sling throws stones.
  - A knife gets more meat and hide from a kill.
- **Cord and glue:** fiber cord with tar or resin binds a head to a handle, so a tool
  lasts longer.
- **Pit traps:** dig a pit and cover it with fronds. This is how a small person brings
  down a giant.
- **Places to work:**
  - The knapping stone takes the place of the workbench.
  - The hearth takes the place of the kiln.
  - The clay pot takes the place of the crate.
  - The fur bed takes the place of the bedroll.
- **Gone:** copper, iron ore, bars, glass, planks, loaves and cakes.
- **Food:** meat, eggs, fish, roots and nuts. Some seeds are poison until cooked.

## Fire

1. **Find it:** at lava, where a star fell, or as a gift from a camp.
2. **Carry it:** a torch burns down, and an ember pot holds fire for a day.
3. **Make it:** first with a fire drill, slowly, and later with flint and firestone, at
   once.

A hearth must be fed or it goes out.

## The mountain and the falling stars

- **The fire mountain** is a cone some 80 blocks wide with a lake of lava in its crater.
  Lava glows and burns. Most days it only smokes.
  - Before it erupts the ground trembles and the beasts leave.
  - When it erupts, burning rocks arc out and land around it, ash darkens the sky, and
    fresh obsidian is left on its sides.
- **Falling stars.** Small ones streak the sky every night. Now and then one comes down
  near you.
  - A fireball lights the land. It strikes, and the boom reaches you a second later for
    each 40 blocks, so you can count how far.
  - It leaves a bowl in the ground, fires, glass, and a core of sky iron too hot to touch
    at first.
- **The Skyspear.** The comet is in the sky from the first night and grows.
  - Stars fall more often as it nears.
  - On the night it arrives comes the Great Fall, and after it the Long Winter: ash in
    the sky, cold in every land, thin herds and bold hunters.
  - Then spring, new craters full of sky iron, and far off the next comet.

## Tame and ride, painting, a tribe

- **Tame and ride.** There are no knock-out timers and no grind.
  - A mossback follows you after three meals from your hand.
  - Bigger beasts start as an egg stolen from a guarded nest, kept warm by your hearth
    and raised.
  - You ride buglers (fast), shieldbrows (they ram) and snowtusks (cold lands). A
    galewing carries you in a glide from high places.
- **Cave painting.** Grind ochre, charcoal and chalk, and paint on stone: hands, spirals,
  the comet, and every beast you've met. A painted wall is the record of what you know.
- **A tribe.** Other people live in camps set by the seed.
  - They talk in pictures over their heads.
  - You trade by showing things. Trust grows with gifts and help.
  - Some come to live at your fire and work: gather, knap, guard, keep the fire.
  - At night they drum and play a bone flute, which is the game's music.

## How it fits the code

- **Beasts need a table.** Today each kind is hand-coded: there's no kind type, and size
  and speed are constants (`internal/game/creature.go`, `gloam.go`). One table row per
  kind comes first, with a few body plans: four legs, two legs, long neck, flyer,
  swimmer, crawler. The shared physics in `internal/game/body.go` already takes any size.
- **Nothing flies yet.** One new kind of moving thing serves the thrown spear, the sling
  stone, the burning rock and the falling star.
- **Land features bigger than a chunk** need a coarse grid hashed from the seed. It's a
  pure function, so a chunk still needs no other chunk (`internal/world/gen.go` has
  `lattice` and `floorDiv` for it). The sky uses the same grid to draw far smoke.
- **Many blocks at once is cheap.** `world.Set` already lights hundreds of changes in one
  pass, so a crater costs little. A star must land inside the loaded land.
- **Third person** needs a second way to build the camera in `Player.Camera()`, a body
  mesh, and aim taken from the camera but measured from the player.
- **Room:** blocks fit 255 and 39 are used. Tiles fit 255 and 74 are used, so tiles are
  the tight one.
- **Big beasts need bigger pictures** than one 16-pixel tile a face.
- **Fire that burns and goes out** needs blocks that change with time. Nothing does that
  today.
- **Saves:** a new version with no way back. Old worlds are listed but don't open.

## The road

Each milestone ends in a playable game.

**Act 1: the first night**

0. **Turn the page.** Tag the block-builder as `v0.1.0`, put this design in the repo, and
   rename to Skyspear.
1. **The old land.** Ferns for grass, new trees, four lands, great skeletons.
2. **The cave person.** The body, the view from behind, crouching.
3. **Beasts.** The table; the mossback moved onto it and tamed by feeding; bugler herds,
   quickclaws, nippers; eyes at night. The gloam goes.
4. **Flint and fire.** The new tools and weapons, the thrown spear, meat and hides, fire
   found, carried and made, the hearth, pots.

After Act 1 the core game exists.

**Act 2: the world's dangers**

5. **Giants.** Shieldbrow, thornback, boulderback, cloudgrazer, the dread; the shaking
   ground; pit traps; stampedes.
6. **The fire mountain.** Lava, obsidian, springs, geysers, lava tubes, smoke on the
   horizon, eruptions.
7. **Falling stars.** Falls, craters, sky iron; then the Skyspear, the Great Fall and the
   Long Winter, with warmth and furs.
8. **More lands.** Coal swamp, mammoth steppe, tar pits, the sea and the sky, with
   beasts that swim and fly.

**Act 3: your people**

9. **Eggs and riding.**
10. **Cave painting.**
11. **A tribe.**
12. **Last polish.** Balance, the README, new pictures.

**Why this order:** the first four give one whole night of the real game early, so a
wrong turn is found before twelve milestones lean on it.

## Open questions

Each is settled when its milestone starts. The first choice named is the one leaned to.

- What the Great Fall leaves behind: winter, then spring, and the comet returns.
- What happens when a tamed beast falls: it limps home and heals.
- How a new cave person looks, and whether the player chooses.
- How near a star may fall to what the player built: never within 24 blocks of the bed.

## Risks

- **It's large:** 13 milestones, each leaning on the one before.
- **Worlds may not last a milestone.** When the save format changes, a world must be made
  again. This holds until the road ends.
- **Giants are hard:**
  - Their pictures can blur.
  - Their body is a square box that can't turn.
  - Beasts that get stuck on land are a top complaint in such games.
- **Fire and eruptions are not a fluid.** Lava doesn't flow, and fire spreads only a
  little, under a cap.

## What the design draws on

- Iron from meteorites was worked cold, long before smelting:
  [Meteoric iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteoric_iron).
- A fireball's boom comes long after its flash:
  [Chelyabinsk meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk_meteor).
- Fire was found and kept long before it could be made:
  [Control of fire by early humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans).
- Tar glued stone to wood at least 191,000 years ago:
  [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1907828116).
- The crest of Parasaurolophus gives a deep horn tone, and the big hunters more likely
  rumbled than roared:
  [what dinosaurs sounded like](https://earthlymission.com/what-dinosaurs-sounded-like-voice-science-listen/).
- Land forms of a volcano:
  [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/volc/types.html).
